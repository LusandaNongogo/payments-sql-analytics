# Payments Operations Review: July To September 2026

A quarter of payments data for a payment service provider with 60 merchants: 49 566 payment attempts, R102.7 million in successful payments and a 90.8% success rate. All figures come from the SQL in `sql/`.

## 1. Reconciliation: About R319 000 Needs Action

Matching our ledger to the acquiring bank's settlement file found 170 exceptions.

| Exception | Items | Value | Action |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Late settlement (after T+1) | 60 | R138 198 | Log against the acquirer service level |
| Missing in bank file | 40 | R97 598 | Query the acquirer |
| Duplicate in bank file | 30 | R58 230 | Recover before paying merchants |
| Missing in our ledger | 15 | R24 160 | Trace; possible unrecorded sales |
| Amount mismatch | 25 | R617 | Compare authorisation and capture amounts |

27 of the 40 payments missing from the bank file are more than 30 days old (R57 282). Those are the priority: the longer an item is unsettled, the harder it is to recover. The duplicates are a direct overpayment risk if merchant payouts are made from the bank file without matching.

## 2. Failed Payments: Bank F Has A Technical Problem

| Issuing bank | Card failure rate | Share of failures that are timeouts |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Bank F | 13.9% | 45% |
| Banks A to E | 7.9% to 9.0% | 5% to 7% |

Bank F's failure rate is not driven by customers being declined. Almost half its failures are timeouts, which points to a connection or integration problem rather than credit risk. The same pattern shows on instant EFT (17.5%, with 54% timeouts). Raising it with the acquirer and Bank F is the single largest lever on success rate.

## 3. Retries: Some Failures Are Worth Retrying, Some Are Not

| Failure reason | Recovered by retry within 15 minutes |
| --- | ---: |
| Timeout | 54.0% |
| 3DS failed | 22.7% |
| Invalid card | 15.7% |
| Issuer declined | 4.8% |
| Insufficient funds | 3.5% |
| Suspected fraud | 1.4% |

Customers who hit a timeout and try again usually succeed. An automatic retry on timeouts, and a clearer "please try again" message after a 3DS failure, would recover sales that are currently lost. Retrying insufficient funds or suspected fraud is pointless and, for fraud, risky.

## 4. Chargebacks: Five Merchants Above 1%

Five merchants with at least 100 card sales have a chargeback rate above 1%, the level at which card schemes start monitoring. The top three run at 2.3% to 4.3%, and around 80% of their chargebacks are card not present fraud.

| Reason | Chargebacks | Merchant win rate | Value lost |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Fraud, card not present | 93 | 18% | R234 126 |
| Goods not received | 32 | 50% | R39 619 |
| Not as described | 17 | 18% | R24 406 |

Fraud chargebacks are both the most common and the hardest to win. For the flagged merchants the fix is upstream: enforce 3DS on card not present payments and review their fraud rules, rather than fighting disputes one by one.

## 5. Volume

Value peaks at month end, when salaries land, at roughly 40% above mid month levels. Settlement cash flow and operations staffing should plan for the last week of each month.

## Recommendations

1. Escalate the 27 unsettled items older than 30 days and the 30 duplicates to the acquirer this week.
2. Open an incident with Bank F on timeouts, and add automatic retry on timeouts.
3. Put the five flagged merchants on enhanced fraud monitoring and require 3DS for their card not present sales.
4. Run the reconciliation daily (`sql/views.sql`, `v_recon_exceptions`) instead of at month end.

## Data Note

The data is synthetic, generated by `src/generate_data.py`, with reconciliation breaks planted on purpose so the SQL can be tested against a known answer. Bank names are placeholders.
